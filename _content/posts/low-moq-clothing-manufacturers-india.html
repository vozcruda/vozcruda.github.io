What MOQ means in clothing manufacturing

MOQ (minimum order quantity) is the smallest order a manufacturer will accept for one product. In apparel it is usually set per style, and often per colour too. A factory quoting "300 pieces MOQ, 100 per colour" means you need at least three colours of 100 pieces each.

Factories are not being difficult. Every production run has costs that stay the same whether they make 50 pieces or 5,000:

  • Line setup: grading patterns, setting markers, adjusting machines and briefing tailors.
  • Fabric minimums: mills and traders sell by the roll or by a minimum dye lot, so small orders leave expensive leftovers.
  • Dyeing and printing: a dye batch, a set of screens or an embroidery program costs the same regardless of quantity.
  • Trims: custom labels, tags and packaging are sold in bulk lots.
  • Overheads: sampling, paperwork and quality checks for every order.

Below the MOQ, those fixed costs are spread over too few pieces. The factory either loses money or quotes a price you cannot sell at.

Typical MOQs by clothing category in India

Many factories also set a per-colour minimum. If you want a tee in four colours, check whether the MOQ applies to the style or to each colour.

When each route makes sense

  • Print-on-demand works for testing designs with zero stock risk, but rarely builds a premium brand.
  • Job-work units suit fully custom pieces in very small numbers, if you can supervise quality closely.
  • Pooling suits brands that want factory quality and near-bulk pricing on proven product types, like heavyweight tees, hoodies, joggers or basic shirts.

How order pooling works

Order pooling, also called MOQ aggregation, lets several buyers share one production run. The factory sees one order that meets its minimum. Each buyer pays for, and receives, only their share.

Here is a simple example. A Tiruppur knitwear unit has an MOQ of 500 pieces for a 240 GSM oversized cotton tee. Your brand needs 120. Alone, you are 380 pieces short and most factories will say no. In a pooled lot, three other buyers add 80, 120 and 180 pieces. The lot reaches 500, production goes ahead, and every brand pays a per-piece price close to the bulk rate.

What stays shared and what stays yours

In a pooled lot the base product is shared: the garment type, fabric, GSM and construction. Your quantity, size split and, depending on the lot, colours, prints and labels can be your own. That is what keeps setup costs shared while your product still looks like your brand.

How to order low MOQ clothing step by step

  1. Define the product clearly. Garment type, fabric and GSM, fit, sizes, colours and any print or embroidery. A simple tech pack helps. See our beginner's tech pack guide.
  2. Pick your quantity per style. Start with what you can sell in 6 to 10 weeks. Reorder winners instead of guessing big.
  3. Choose your route. Under about 300 pieces per style, a pooled lot on Smallotz is usually the most cost-effective. Above that, Voz Cruda can match you with a factory directly.
  4. Approve a sample. Check fabric hand-feel, measurements, stitching and print quality before bulk production.
  5. Confirm in writing. Quantity, size split, price, delivery date and quality standard on a purchase order, not only a WhatsApp chat.
  6. Inspect on arrival and reorder. Count, measure and check a sample of pieces. Then reorder the styles that sold.

Questions to ask any low MOQ supplier

  • Is the MOQ per style, per colour, or per fabric?
  • What is the price at my quantity, and what would it be at 2x and 5x?
  • How much does a sample cost, and is it adjusted against the bulk order?
  • What is the lead time after sample approval?
  • What happens if pieces fail measurements or have defects?
  • Can I reorder the same product later at the same quantity?
