Short answerTo negotiate a lower MOQ, find out what is driving the minimum and remove it. Use the factory's stock fabric, cut down on colours, share one fabric across styles, keep trims standard, offer a reorder plan or a flexible delivery date, or accept a modest surcharge. If none of that works, pool your order with other buyers so the lot meets the factory minimum.

New brands often treat an MOQ like a price tag: take it or leave it. In practice it is the factory's way of protecting itself from fixed costs it cannot spread over a small order. If your request avoids those costs, or covers them another way, the number usually has room to move.

If you are new to the term, start with our guide to low MOQ clothing manufacturing in India, which explains the different kinds of minimum and why factories set them.

First, ask what the MOQ is based on

Before negotiating anything, ask one question: "Is this minimum for the stitching, the fabric, the print, or the trims?" The answer tells you where to push.

9 ways to get a lower MOQ

1. Choose stock or running fabric

Custom-dyed fabric often has its own minimum from the dyeing unit or mill, and that minimum then sets yours. Ask which fabrics and colours the factory already runs. Black, white, navy, grey melange and a few seasonal shades are commonly available in basic knits, which can remove the fabric minimum entirely.

2. Order fewer colours

Per-colour minimums multiply fast. Three colours at 100 pieces each is 300 pieces; one colour at 150 might be accepted. Launch in your best colour and add others when it sells.

3. Use one fabric across several styles

If a tee and a longline tee, or a hoodie and a sweatshirt, share the same fleece, the factory can buy one larger fabric lot. Some factories will then accept lower quantities per style because the fabric total meets their minimum.

4. Keep trims and labels standard for the first run

Custom zips, woven labels, branded drawcord tips and special buttons each carry their own minimums and lead times. A printed neck label and standard trims for your first order keep things moving. Upgrade once the style has proved itself.

5. Simplify the print

In screen printing, each colour usually needs its own screen and setup. A one or two colour design is cheaper to set up than a six colour one. For small quantities of complex artwork, ask whether DTF suits the design instead. Our comparison of screen printing vs DTF vs DTG explains the trade-offs.

6. Be flexible on timing

Factories have busy and quiet periods. If your order can slot into a gap between larger orders, a smaller quantity is easier to accept. Saying "we are flexible on the delivery date by two or three weeks" is a real concession.

7. Offer a clear reorder plan

A factory takes a small first order more seriously when it can see the next one. Share your plan honestly, for example "120 pieces now, and we expect to reorder this style within three months if it sells." Do not promise volumes you cannot commit to; factories remember.

8. Accept a small surcharge

Many factories will produce below MOQ for a higher price per piece, which covers setup costs over fewer units. Before you agree, check that your margin still works using our clothing pricing guide. A surcharge that is fine on a premium hoodie may break the numbers on a basic tee.

9. Pool your order with other buyers

If the factory will not move, change who makes up the minimum. In a pooled lot, several brands order the same base product, and the combined quantity meets the factory MOQ. Each brand pays near-bulk pricing for only its own quantity. Read how MOQ aggregation works.

A message you can send

Keep it short, specific and easy to say yes to. Factories get many vague enquiries, and a clear one stands out.

TemplateHello, we are a new brand based in [city] looking to make [product], [fabric and GSM], in [colours]. Our first order is [quantity] pieces across sizes [S to XL]. We understand your usual minimum is higher. To make a smaller order work, we can use one of your running fabrics and colours, keep trims standard, and be flexible on delivery by two to three weeks. If it sells, we plan to reorder this style. Could you let us know the lowest quantity you could accept on those terms, and the price at that quantity and at your standard MOQ? Our tech pack is attached.

Attaching a tech pack shows you are serious and lets the factory quote accurately. If you do not have one yet, our tech pack guide and t-shirt tech pack example will help.

What not to do

  • Do not ask for bulk pricing at sample quantities. Ask for the price at your quantity and at their MOQ, so you can see the real difference.
  • Do not hide your quantity. Factories quote better when they know the real number.
  • Do not promise future volumes you cannot commit to.
  • Do not skip the sample to save money on a small order. A rejected bulk order costs far more. See the sampling process.
  • Do not negotiate only on WhatsApp. Confirm the agreed quantity, price, size split and date on a purchase order.

When to stop negotiating

If the price at your quantity makes the product unprofitable, or the factory agrees but shows little interest in your order, it may be the wrong fit. A factory that sees your order as a nuisance tends to slot it last. It is usually better to find a smaller unit, a factory with a running product close to yours, or a pooled lot.
