Short answerGarment manufacturers in India find buyers mainly through referrals, B2B marketplaces, trade fairs, buying agents and sourcing partners, and direct outreach to brands whose products match their capability. Bought lists of "garment buyer contact details" rarely work. A clear capability sheet sent to the right brands works much better.

Most of our guides are written for brands looking for a factory. This one is for the other side of the table: the factory owner, merchant or production head who has capacity and wants more of the right orders. It draws on what buyers tell us they look for when choosing a manufacturer.

Why buyer contact lists rarely work

It is tempting to buy a spreadsheet of thousands of "garment buyers" and email them all. In practice:

  • The contacts are often outdated or generic inboxes that nobody in sourcing reads.
  • They are rarely the decision maker. At small brands the founder decides; at larger ones a sourcing or production manager does.
  • Mass emails look like spam, and sending them can hurt your email domain's reputation.
  • Most of the brands will not match what you make. A knitwear unit gets nowhere pitching a denim brand.

Twenty well-chosen brands contacted personally usually do more than two thousand cold emails.

Know which buyers suit your unit

Before looking for buyers, write down who you are actually good for. It makes every channel below work better.

8 channels that bring in real orders

1. Referrals from current customers

Happy buyers are your best salespeople. Ask them directly who else they know who is launching or scaling, and deliver well on small orders: small brands talk to each other constantly.

2. B2B marketplaces and directories

Platforms such as IndiaMART and TradeIndia are where many domestic buyers start searching. A listing works when it is specific: real photos of your work, products and fabrics you make, MOQ and a fast response. Generic listings with stock photos get ignored.

3. Trade fairs and industry events

Apparel and textile fairs, including events organised by export promotion bodies such as AEPC, put you in front of buyers who are actively sourcing. Bring samples, a printed capability sheet and a clear answer to "what is your MOQ?"

4. Buying agents and buying houses

Agents and buying houses connect brands, often international, with factories. They take a commission or margin, but can bring steady volume if your compliance and quality match their buyers' needs.

5. Sourcing partners and pooled-order platforms

Sourcing companies match brands with factories and coordinate production. Pooled-order platforms go further for small buyers: they combine many small orders into one that meets your MOQ, so you get factory-sized orders from brands that could never place one alone. 6. Direct outreach to brands that fit

Look at Instagram, marketplaces and D2C websites for brands selling the kind of product you make well. Find the founder or sourcing contact on their website or LinkedIn, and send a short, personal message (template below).

7. Your own website and Google Business Profile

Many buyers search on Google for terms like "t-shirt manufacturer in Tiruppur" or "kurta manufacturer Jaipur". A simple website with your products, capacity, photos and contact details, plus a complete Google Business Profile with real photos, helps you appear in those searches.

8. Wholesale markets and local networks

For domestic wholesale and retail buyers, local markets, associations and traders in your hub remain important. They often lead to steady repeat business on running styles.

The capability sheet buyers want

A one or two page PDF that answers a buyer's first questions before they ask. Include:

  • What you make: product types, fabrics and GSM ranges, and what you do not make.
  • Processes in-house vs outsourced: knitting, dyeing, cutting, stitching, printing, embroidery, finishing.
  • MOQ: per style and per colour, and whether you accept pooled or smaller orders.
  • Capacity and lead times: realistic monthly capacity and typical time from sample approval to dispatch.
  • Sampling: sample cost, time, and whether it is adjusted against bulk.
  • Photos: your actual unit and finished pieces, not stock images.
  • Certifications and compliance, if you have them, such as GST registration and any audits or fabric certifications.
  • A named contact with phone, WhatsApp and email.

A message you can send to a brand

TemplateHello [name], I am [your name] from [unit name] in [city]. We make [products] in [fabrics and GSM range], and work with brands ordering from [your MOQ] pieces per style. I noticed your [product] range and think our [specific capability] could suit it. I have attached a short capability sheet with photos of our recent work. Happy to send a sample of something similar if useful.

Keep it short, mention something specific about their brand, and offer a sample instead of a long pitch.

Win the reorder, not just the first order

  • Share a dated production plan and update it without being chased.
  • Flag problems early. Buyers forgive a delay they hear about in advance far more than one they discover.
  • Match the approved sample in bulk. Quiet substitutions in fabric or trims lose buyers.
  • Pack and label exactly as asked. It is the first thing the buyer sees when the carton opens.
