Short answerTiruppur, in western Tamil Nadu near Coimbatore, is India's best-known hub for cotton knitwear: t-shirts, polos, hoodies, sweatshirts, joggers, innerwear and kidswear. It works as a cluster of specialised units for knitting, dyeing, printing, stitching and packing, coordinated by a manufacturer or merchant. Small brands can source there through smaller units, a sourcing partner, or pooled orders that meet a factory's minimum.

Our overview of India's garment hubs explains what each city specialises in. This guide goes deeper on the one most small apparel brands start with, because almost every new label's first product is a tee or a hoodie.

How the Tiruppur cluster works

A buyer usually deals with one manufacturer, but the garment often passes through several specialised units before it is finished. Understanding this helps you ask better questions.

  1. Yarn is bought from spinning mills.
  2. Knitting units turn yarn into greige (undyed) fabric on circular knitting machines.
  3. Dyeing and processing units dye, wash and finish the fabric, including compacting to control shrinkage.
  4. Cutting and stitching happen at the manufacturer's own unit or with a job-work stitching unit.
  5. Printing and embroidery are often done by separate specialist units.
  6. Checking, ironing and packing finish the order.

Some larger factories do several of these steps in-house. Many smaller manufacturers coordinate job-work units for the rest. Neither is automatically better. What matters is that the person you deal with controls quality across every step.

Ask this"Which steps do you do in-house, and which go to job-work units?" A clear answer shows the manufacturer knows its own supply chain. It also tells you where delays are most likely, since each handover adds time.

MOQs and pricing in Tiruppur

Many Tiruppur units are built for export and large domestic orders, so minimums of several hundred pieces per style are common, often with per-colour minimums too. The ranges in our low MOQ guide are a good guide to what you will hear.

The biggest drivers of price per piece are:

  • Fabric: yarn quality (combed or carded cotton), GSM and any special finish such as bio-wash.
  • Colour: running colours are cheaper and faster than custom dyed shades.
  • Print or embroidery: method, size and number of colours.
  • Quantity: setup costs spread across fewer pieces push the price up.
  • Trims and packing: custom labels, tags and packaging.

Small brands often get the best results by choosing a running fabric and colour, keeping the first print simple, and pooling quantity with other buyers. Our guide on how to negotiate MOQ covers more tactics.

Lead times

For basic tees and fleece, plan for sampling first, then several weeks of production after you approve the sample. Lead times stretch when the fabric needs a custom dye, when artwork needs several sampling rounds, or during peak seasons when units are full with large orders. Ask for a dated plan with fabric, sample approval, production and dispatch milestones. Our sampling guide explains each stage.

Sourcing from Tiruppur remotely

You do not need to visit to work with a Tiruppur manufacturer. A remote process that works:

  1. Send a clear tech pack with your quantity and target date.
  2. Ask for fabric swatches or a photo and GSM reading of the running fabric.
  3. Approve a sample by courier, measured against your size chart.
  4. Get a video walk-through of the unit and a mid-production update with photos.
  5. Check a sample of finished pieces before final payment, or use a third-party inspection.

Delivery within India is usually by road transport or courier. Agree who arranges and pays for it before production starts.

If you do visit

  • Fly into Coimbatore and travel to Tiruppur by road.
  • Bring your tech pack and reference garments. Touching fabric in person is the fastest way to agree a quality level.
  • Visit more than one unit, including the stitching floor, not only the office and showroom.
  • Look for organised cutting tables, measurement checks on the line, clean storage of finished goods and a quality checker with your spec sheet.
  • Ask to see a current order for another brand at a similar quantity to yours.

Is Tiruppur right for your brand?

If your range is built around cotton knits, such as tees, polos, sweatshirts, hoodies, joggers or kidswear, Tiruppur is usually the right place to start. If you mainly make woven shirts, denim, structured womenswear or ethnic wear, another hub will usually suit you better.
