Short answerA private label clothing brand sells garments made by a manufacturer under your own brand name. You start from a proven product, customise the details that matter (fabric, colour, fit, print, labels, packaging) and sell it as yours. It is faster and cheaper than designing every piece from scratch, and in India it can now start at low MOQ.

Private label vs white label vs custom design

These three terms get mixed up. The difference is how much of the product you change.

ModelWhat you changeSpeed to launchBrand uniqueness
White labelOnly your label and packagingFastestLow
Private labelLabels plus fabric, colour, print, fit tweaksFastMedium to high
Full custom designEverything, from pattern to fabricSlowestHighest

Most successful small brands start with private label on a few hero products, then move into custom design once they know what their customers want.

Step 1: Pick your hero products

Choose two or three products your niche buys again and again. For most streetwear and casual brands that means heavyweight tees, hoodies, sweatshirts or joggers. For womenswear it could be co-ord sets, kurtas or shirts. Fewer products means lower MOQ pressure and a clearer brand.

Step 2: Decide what to customise

  • Fabric and GSM: for example, 240 GSM cotton for a premium oversized tee.
  • Fit: oversized, boxy, regular or cropped. Small changes to body length and sleeve make a big difference.
  • Colour: your signature shades.
  • Decoration: screen print, DTF print, puff print or embroidery.
  • Branding: woven neck labels or printed neck labels, wash care labels, hang tags and packaging.

Step 3: Find the right private label manufacturer

Match the product to the region that specialises in it. Tiruppur leads in knitwear like tees and hoodies; Surat in synthetics and wovens; Jaipur in ethnic wear and prints; Delhi NCR in fashion and private label. Read our guide to India's apparel hubs for more.

When you speak to manufacturers, confirm:

  • They make your exact product category regularly.
  • MOQ per style and per colour, plus minimums for labels and tags.
  • Sampling cost and time.
  • Whether they can source your fabric or need you to supply it.

Step 4: Build your brand kit

Private label lives or dies on how branded it feels. Prepare:

  1. Logo files in vector format for labels and prints.
  2. Neck label design, with size and fibre content.
  3. Wash care label with fibre composition and care instructions.
  4. Hang tag and price tag design.
  5. Packaging: polybag, mailer box or tissue and sticker.

Check the labelling requirements that apply to your products and channels, such as fibre content, size, maker details and MRP for retail sales.

Step 5: Sample, then produce

Approve a pre-production sample with your exact fabric, print and labels. Compare it against your tech pack measurements. Only then confirm bulk production on a written purchase order with quantity, size split, price and delivery date.

Step 6: Launch and reorder

Shoot strong product photos, launch on Instagram and your own store, and track which sizes and colours sell. Reorder winners fast. Once a style regularly sells a few hundred units per run, you can move it from pooled lots to a direct factory order through Voz Cruda.

Private label launch checklist

  • Niche and 2 to 3 hero products chosen
  • Customisation decided: fabric, GSM, fit, colours, decoration
  • Manufacturer or pooled lot selected
  • Brand kit ready: logo, labels, tags, packaging
  • Sample approved against tech pack
  • Purchase order confirmed in writing
  • Photos, store and launch plan ready
