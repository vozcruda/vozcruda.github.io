Short answerClothing sampling usually moves from a proto sample (first version) to a fit sample (adjusted fit), sometimes a size set, and finally a pre-production sample made with exact bulk materials. Each round often takes one to three weeks. A clear tech pack and structured feedback are the fastest ways to fewer rounds.

Types of samples

SamplePurposeWhat to check
Proto sampleFirst version of the designOverall look, construction, proportions
Fit sampleRefine fit on a real bodyMeasurements, comfort, drape
Size setCheck grading across sizesEach size against the size chart
Pre-production (PP) sampleFinal approval with bulk materialsFabric, colour, print, labels, finishing
Top of production (TOP)Pieces pulled from early bulkBulk matches the PP sample

Small brands often combine steps, for example going from proto straight to PP for a simple tee with proven measurements.

How to brief a sample

  1. Send a tech pack with measurements, fabric, GSM and artwork.
  2. Include a reference garment if you have one with the fit you want.
  3. Confirm sample cost, timeline and whether it adjusts against bulk.
  4. Agree measurement tolerances upfront.

How to review a sample

  • Measure every point and record differences in a table.
  • Try it on people of your target size. Check shoulders, chest, length and sleeve.
  • Check fabric: GSM, hand-feel, colour.
  • Inspect construction: seams, neck finish, hems, rib.
  • Check decoration: placement, colours, sharpness.
  • Wash it three to five times for shrinkage, bleeding and print durability.

Give feedback that gets fixed

Vague comments like "make it more oversized" lead to more rounds. Give measured changes instead:

VagueClear
Make it more oversizedChest width +4 cm in all sizes, drop shoulder by 3 cm
Print looks too lowMove print up to 7 cm below neck seam
Fabric feels thinChange to 240 GSM combed cotton
Neck looks offRib width 2 cm, reduce neck drop by 1 cm

Mark changes on photos, update the tech pack version and send both together.

Costs and timelines

Samples cost more per piece than bulk because each is cut and sewn individually, sometimes from fabric bought in small quantities. Each round commonly takes one to three weeks; custom fabric, dyeing or embroidery adds time. Budget for at least two rounds on a new style.

How pooled lots shorten sampling

In a pooled lot the base garment, fabric and construction are already developed and shared. You mainly review the fit, fabric and your own print or labels, instead of developing a pattern from scratch. That can save rounds, time and sampling cost on your first drop.

Common sampling mistakes

  • Approving a sample without washing it.
  • Changing several things at once without measurements.
  • Skipping the PP sample, then finding bulk fabric or colour is different.
  • Not keeping the approved sample as the reference for bulk checks.
