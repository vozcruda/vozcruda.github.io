Short answerChoose fabric by product and climate: cotton single jersey for tees, fleece or French terry for hoodies, linen, cotton or rayon for summer wovens, and polyester blends for activewear. GSM (grams per square metre) tells you the weight; for example, about 160 to 200 GSM for regular tees and 220 to 260 GSM for heavyweight oversized tees. Always confirm composition and GSM in writing and check them on the sample.

Common knit fabrics

  • Single jersey: the classic t-shirt fabric, smooth on one side.
  • Rib: stretchy with visible ridges; used for cuffs, collars and fitted tops.
  • Interlock: thicker, smooth on both sides, stable; popular for baby wear.
  • Pique: textured, breathable; used for polo shirts.
  • French terry: loops on the inside; for year-round sweatshirts and joggers.
  • Fleece: brushed on the inside for warmth; for winter hoodies.

Common woven fabrics

  • Poplin and cambric: smooth, crisp cotton for shirts and kurtas.
  • Twill: diagonal weave, durable; for chinos and workwear.
  • Denim: sturdy cotton twill for jeans and jackets.
  • Linen: very breathable with a natural texture; wrinkles easily.
  • Rayon (viscose): soft and drapey; popular for kurtas and dresses.
  • Mulmul and voile: very light cottons for summer wear.
  • Georgette and crepe: flowy synthetics or blends for festive and western wear.

Cotton quality terms

  • Carded vs combed: combed cotton removes short fibres for a smoother, stronger fabric.
  • Bio-washed: enzyme-treated for softness and less pilling.
  • Compacted or pre-shrunk: processed to reduce shrinkage after washing.
  • Organic: grown without synthetic pesticides and fertilisers; ask for certification if you market it.
  • Yarn count: higher counts mean finer yarn and a smoother fabric.

Fabric checks before bulk production

  • Weigh a swatch or sample to confirm GSM.
  • Wash-test for shrinkage, colour bleeding and twisting.
  • Rub-test dark colours for colour transfer.
  • Check that bulk uses the same fabric lot as the approved sample.
