Short answerTo start a clothing brand in India on a small budget: pick a narrow niche, launch 2 to 4 styles, produce them in small quantities through a low MOQ route such as order pooling, sell through Instagram and your own store first, and reinvest in reorders of the styles that sell. Stock is your biggest cost, so keeping your first drop small keeps your investment small.

The most common way new fashion brands run out of money is not bad design. It is buying too much stock too early. A factory asks for 500 pieces per style, the founder orders three styles, and suddenly most of the budget is sitting in cartons. This plan is built to avoid that.

Step 1: Choose a niche you can describe in one line

"Clothing for everyone" is impossible to market on a small budget. A narrow niche makes every rupee of marketing work harder. Good niches combine a customer, a product and a reason to buy:

  • Heavyweight oversized basics for college students in tier-2 cities.
  • Breathable co-ord sets for working women in humid cities.
  • Minimal graphic tees for indie music fans.

Check that people already buy in that space: look at Instagram pages, marketplace reviews and what competitors restock.

Step 2: Keep your first drop small

Launch 2 to 4 styles in 2 or 3 colours each. A small drop is cheaper to produce, easier to shoot and gives you clean data. If one style sells out and another sits, you have learned something worth more than a big collection.

Rule of thumbOrder what you can realistically sell in 6 to 10 weeks. Reordering a winner is cheaper than discounting dead stock.

Step 3: Get the product right before you buy stock

  1. Write a simple tech pack: sketches, measurements per size, fabric, GSM, colours and print placement. Our tech pack guide walks through it.
  2. Order samples: expect one or two rounds. Wear-test and wash-test them.
  3. Fix the size chart: fit problems are the top reason for returns in online fashion.

Step 5: Budget for more than production

Founders often budget for stock and forget everything around it. Plan a line for each of these:

  • Samples and tech pack: sampling rounds, courier and any design help.
  • Production: pieces, labels, tags, polybags and boxes.
  • Brand basics: logo, trademark application through IP India, website and domain.
  • Photography: product and on-model shots. This sells more than any ad.
  • Marketing: creator collaborations and a small paid ads test.
  • Operations: shipping partner, payment gateway fees and returns.
  • Buffer: keep money aside for the reorder of your best seller.

To turn these lines into a selling price, see how to price your clothing products.

Step 6: Set up the business basics

  • Choose a structure: sole proprietorship, partnership, LLP or private limited company.
  • Open a business bank account.
  • Check GST registration requirements with a chartered accountant, especially before selling on marketplaces.
  • Register on the Udyam portal to be recognised as an MSME.
  • Search and file your brand name as a trademark early.

Step 7: Sell where your customers already are

  • Instagram first: build interest before launch with behind-the-scenes posts from sampling and production.
  • Your own store: a Shopify or similar store keeps your margin and customer data.
  • Marketplaces later: Myntra, Ajio and others add reach but come with commissions and return costs.
  • Pop-ups and flea markets: great for feedback and cash flow in your own city.

Step 8: Reorder fast, expand slowly

Track sell-through by style, colour and size. When something sells, reorder it quickly, in a pooled lot or directly once volumes grow. Add new styles one or two at a time. Brands that grow on reorders keep their cash moving and avoid the dead-stock trap.
