Short answerBengaluru is one of India's long-established garment manufacturing hubs, best known for woven garments: shirts, trousers, dresses, women's wear and denim, much of it made for export brands. Large export factories usually have high MOQs, while smaller domestic units in and around the city work with Indian brands. For cotton knit basics like t-shirts and hoodies, Tiruppur is usually the better fit.

How the Bengaluru cluster works

Bengaluru's garment industry grew around large export factories that make woven garments for international fashion brands. Around them sits a wider network of smaller units, job-work tailoring units, washing and finishing units, embroidery and printing shops, and trim suppliers.

For a buyer, there are broadly two kinds of factory:

Large export factoriesSmaller domestic units
Typical buyersInternational brands and large retailersIndian brands, D2C labels, boutiques
MOQsHigh, often per colourLower and more flexible
SystemsFormal compliance, audits, detailed quality systemsVaries widely; vet carefully
FlexibilityLower for small ordersHigher; more willing to sample and adjust

Most small brands get the best results from a capable domestic unit, or by reaching an export-grade factory through a pooled order.

Where garment units are

Garment units are spread across the city's industrial and mixed-use areas rather than in one district. Peenya, the Mysore Road belt and the Hosur Road side are commonly known for garment units, and more factories sit in towns around Bengaluru. Because the cluster is spread out, a sourcing partner or a planned visit with appointments saves a lot of travel time in city traffic.

MOQs and pricing

Woven garments usually involve more parts and operations than a basic tee: collars, cuffs, plackets, buttons, interlining, pockets and sometimes washing. That affects both price and minimums.

  • Fabric: woven fabrics are often bought by the metre with mill or trader minimums. Using a fabric the unit already stocks keeps MOQs down.
  • Trims: buttons, interlining, zips and labels each have their own minimums.
  • Washing: denim and garment-washed pieces need washing runs, which push minimums up.
  • Construction: more operations per garment means a higher making cost.

How to source from Bengaluru

  1. Prepare a tech pack with measurements, fabric, trims and finishing. Woven garments have more details to specify than knits. Start from our free tech pack template.
  2. Shortlist units that already make your product, at a quantity close to yours. Ask to see current work.
  3. Visit or video call the unit, including the stitching floor and finishing area.
  4. Order a paid sample and check it against your tech pack. See the sampling process.
  5. Agree everything in writing: quantity, size split, price, delivery date, quality standard and payment terms.
  6. Check a sample of finished pieces before final payment.

Is Bengaluru right for your brand?

If your range leans on woven shirts, trousers, dresses or denim, Bengaluru deserves a place on your shortlist. If it is built on cotton knit basics, start with Tiruppur.
