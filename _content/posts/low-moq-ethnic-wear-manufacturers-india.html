Short answerTo source ethnic wear in small batches in India, pick your hub by style (Jaipur for block prints, Surat for synthetic and printed fabrics, Delhi NCR for embroidery and fusion), choose a print method that suits small runs such as digital print, and keep your first drop to a few designs in a focused size range. Ethnic wear MOQs are often 100 to 300 pieces per design, and pooled lots can bring that lower.

Kurtas, kurta sets and ethnic co-ords sell all year in India, with festive and wedding peaks on top. Because much of the industry already works in smaller print and dye batches, ethnic wear is one of the easier categories to start small in.

Where to source

  • Jaipur: block-printed cottons, kurtas, dresses and sets with a handcrafted feel. Nearby Sanganer and Bagru are known for traditional prints.
  • Surat: synthetic and blended fabrics, printed dress materials and value ethnic wear.
  • Delhi NCR: embroidery, embellishment and fusion ethnic wear for brands.

Ways to keep MOQs low in ethnic wear

  • Share a base fabric across several designs so fabric minimums are met together.
  • Use digital printing for multi-colour designs without screens.
  • Limit colourways on your first drop.
  • Keep sizes focused, for example S to XXL, before adding plus sizes in a later drop.
  • Pool your order with other buyers using the same base product.

Quality points to check on samples

  • Colour fastness: wash the sample and check for bleeding, especially with deep dyes.
  • Print alignment at seams and panels.
  • Shrinkage on cotton and rayon after washing.
  • Neckline finish, side slits and hem straightness.
  • Lining quality in sheer fabrics.

Plan around the festive calendar

Festive demand peaks around Diwali and the wedding season. Factories get busy in the weeks before, so place festive orders well ahead and keep everyday styles running year-round.
