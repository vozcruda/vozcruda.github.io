Short answerUse screen printing for bold designs with few colours, the best durability and the lowest cost at larger quantities. Use DTF for small batches and multi-colour or detailed designs on most fabrics. Use DTG for photographic, full-colour art on cotton with a soft feel. For small brands, DTF is often the most flexible starting point.

How each method works

  • Screen printing: ink is pushed through a mesh screen onto the fabric, one screen per colour. Setup takes time, then printing is fast.
  • DTF (direct to film): the design is printed onto a film with adhesive powder, then heat-pressed onto the garment.
  • DTG (direct to garment): an inkjet printer sprays ink directly into the fabric, like a paper printer.

How cost changes with quantity

Screen printing has a fixed setup cost for each colour, so the price per piece falls quickly as quantity rises. DTF and DTG have almost no setup, so their price per piece stays fairly flat. That is why DTF and DTG usually win at small quantities, and screen printing wins as orders grow, especially for one or two colour designs.

Rule of thumbMany colours or small quantity: DTF or DTG. Few colours and growing quantity: screen print. Ask your manufacturer to quote both at your quantity.

Other decoration options

  • Puff print: a raised, textured screen print popular in streetwear.
  • Embroidery: premium and durable for logos. Cost depends on stitch count, not colours.
  • Sublimation: all-over prints on white polyester, common in sportswear.
  • Discharge printing: removes dye from the fabric for a very soft vintage feel on cotton.

Check print quality on your sample

  • Sharp edges and correct colours against your artwork.
  • Placement measured from the neck seam, as in your tech pack.
  • No cracking when stretched.
  • Three to five washes without fading or peeling.
