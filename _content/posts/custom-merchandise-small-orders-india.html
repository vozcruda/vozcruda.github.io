Short answerFor custom merchandise in small quantities, pick the product (tees, polos, hoodies or dri-fit), choose a decoration that suits the logo (embroidery for premium logos, print for large or colourful designs), collect sizes from your team, and order early. For very small runs, print on quality blanks; for custom-made pieces, pooled lots on Smallotz avoid full factory minimums.

Who orders custom merch

  • Companies and startups: onboarding kits, offsites, client gifts.
  • Events: conferences, marathons, product launches, volunteers.
  • Colleges and schools: fests, clubs, batch tees, sports teams.
  • Teams and communities: cricket and football teams, running clubs, cafés and restaurants.

Branding options

  • Screen print: cost-effective for simple logos as quantity grows.
  • DTF print: flexible for multi-colour logos and small runs.
  • Embroidery: premium, durable look for polos, caps and hoodies.
  • Sublimation: all-over designs on polyester sportswear.

Common placements are the left chest, centre front, upper back and sleeve. Compare methods in screen printing vs DTF vs DTG.

Plan sizes properly

Wrong sizes are the most common merch complaint. Share a size chart with chest and length measurements, and collect each person's size through a simple form. If you cannot collect sizes, weight the order towards M and L and order a few spares.

Timeline checklist

  1. Finalise logo files in vector format.
  2. Choose products, colours and placements.
  3. Collect sizes.
  4. Approve a digital mock-up and, for larger orders, a physical sample.
  5. Production and delivery. Build in a buffer before the event date.
