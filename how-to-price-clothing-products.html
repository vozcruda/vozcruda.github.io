How to Price Your Clothing Products: Costing, Margins and MRP for Indian Brands

Many new brands price by copying competitors and discover months later they lose money on every order. Here is how to build a price from your real costs up.

Short answerPrice a clothing product from the bottom up: add every cost to get your landed cost per piece, add the costs of selling it (shipping, returns, fees, marketing per order), then apply a markup that leaves real profit. Many D2C brands land at around three to four times landed cost. Finally, check the price against your market and positioning.

Step 1: Build your landed cost per piece

Landed cost is everything it takes to get one finished, packed piece into your hands.

  • Fabric and making (cut, make and trim)
  • Printing or embroidery
  • Labels, hang tags and trims
  • Polybag, mailer box and inserts
  • Freight from the factory to you
  • Sampling and tech pack costs, spread over the order
  • Taxes you cannot recover

Step 2: Add the cost of selling each order

  • Shipping to the customer, including cash-on-delivery charges if you offer COD.
  • Payment gateway fees on prepaid orders.
  • Returns and exchanges: reverse shipping and repacking, spread across kept orders.
  • Marketplace commission and fees if you sell on platforms.
  • Marketing cost per order: ad spend and creator costs divided by orders.

Step 3: A worked example

The figures below are illustrative, to show the method. Use your own quotes.

LinePer piece (₹)
Heavyweight tee, made and printed300

Marketing per order250

Notice how much marketing and fulfilment add. A brand that priced at 2x landed cost (₹720) would lose money on every order once marketing is included.

Step 4: Choose a markup that fits your channel

ChannelWhat eats the marginPricing implication
Your own websiteAds, shipping, payment feesHighest margin if marketing is efficient
MarketplacesCommission, fees, high returnsNeeds a higher multiple or a separate price
Wholesale to boutiquesRetailer's own markupYour wholesale price is often around half of MRP
Pop-ups and offlineStall rent, travelGood margin, low return rate

If you plan to sell wholesale later, set an MRP that leaves room for a retailer's margin from the start.

Step 5: Lower your costs without cutting quality

  • Avoid below-MOQ surcharges by pooling your order. Shared setup costs keep the per-piece price close to bulk.
  • Reduce returns with an accurate size chart and honest photos.
  • Push prepaid orders to cut COD costs and cancellations.
  • Reorder winners instead of carrying slow stock you later discount.

Step 6: Sense-check against the market

Compare your price with brands at a similar quality level, not the cheapest option. If your number is higher, ask whether your fabric, fit, design and brand justify it, and make sure your product pages show why. If it is far lower, you may be leaving money on the table.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good markup for clothing?

Many D2C clothing brands aim to sell at roughly three to four times their landed product cost, so there is room for marketing, returns, platform fees and profit. The right multiple depends on your channel and positioning.

What costs should I include when pricing clothes?

Include fabric and making, printing, labels, packaging, freight to you, sampling spread over the order, taxes, payment gateway fees, shipping to customers, returns, marketplace commissions and marketing cost per order.

How do returns affect clothing pricing?

Returns add reverse shipping, repacking and sometimes unsellable stock. Estimate your return rate and spread that cost across the orders you keep, especially if you sell on marketplaces.

Does ordering in small quantities raise my cost per piece?

Ordering alone below a factory's MOQ usually does. Pooled lots keep the per-piece price close to bulk because setup costs are shared across all buyers in the lot.

Should I price lower than competitors to start?

Underpricing is risky. It leaves no budget for marketing and is hard to raise later. It is usually better to justify your price with product quality, fit and brand.
