Tech Pack Example: A Complete T-Shirt Tech Pack, Page by Page

Templates are easier to use once you have seen one filled in. Here is a full tech pack for an oversized cotton tee, with notes on why each line is there and what to change for your own style.

Short answerA complete t-shirt tech pack has eight parts: a cover page, front and back flat sketches, a size chart with tolerances, a bill of materials, construction details, artwork and placement, labels and packing, and an order sheet. Below is each part filled in for an example oversized cotton tee, which you can adapt for your own style.

Our beginner's guide to tech packs explains what a tech pack is and why it matters. This page is the practical companion: a worked example you can follow line by line. The style, numbers and materials are an illustration for a typical oversized tee, not a standard. Use your own reference garment for your measurements.

Page 1: Cover

BrandYour brand name
Style nameOversized Heavy Tee
Style numberTS-001
Season or dropDrop 01
Version and dateV1, first issue
SizesS, M, L, XL
ContactName, phone and email of the person who approves samples

Why it matters: the style number and version are how you and the factory refer to this exact garment. When a sample comes back, you will review it against "TS-001 V1" and issue "V2" with the changes, so nobody works from an old file.

Page 2: Flat sketches

Two simple line drawings, front and back, drawn as if the tee is lying flat. No model, no shading. Add arrows and short callouts for each detail:

  • Dropped shoulder seam
  • 2 cm 1x1 rib neck, with self-fabric back neck tape
  • Single-needle topstitch at neck rib
  • 2.5 cm twin-needle hem on body and sleeves
  • Front print position (see page 6) and printed neck label position (see page 7)

Why it matters: callouts tell the sampling tailor what is intentional. Without "dropped shoulder" on the sketch, a factory used to regular fits may correct it for you.

All measurements in centimetres, garment measured flat. The numbers below are an example for a boxy oversized fit.

Add a small diagram with letters A to J so the factory's quality checker measures the same way you do.

TipTolerances are tighter on small details like rib width and neck drop, because a 1 cm difference there is easy to see. Body measurements can usually take a little more.

Why it matters: this table is what the finished garments are checked against. "Fits a bit small" is an opinion. "Half chest 55 cm against 58.5 ± 1" is a defect.

Why it matters: GSM, composition and finish change both the feel and the price. "Cotton tee" could be quoted at 160 GSM or 260 GSM. See our fabric and GSM guide if these terms are new.

Page 5: Construction

  • Shoulder seams: 4-thread overlock with tape for stability.
  • Side seams: 4-thread overlock, no side slits.
  • Sleeve attach: 4-thread overlock.
  • Neck: rib attached with overlock, single-needle topstitch on body side.
  • Hems: body and sleeve hems 2.5 cm, twin-needle coverstitch.
  • Stitch density: state your expectation, or ask the factory for its standard and record it here.

Why it matters: hem width and neck finish are some of the most visible signs of quality on a tee, and they are easy to change silently between sample and bulk if they are not written down.

Page 6: Artwork and placement

MethodScreen print, water-based ink
Artwork fileTS-001_front_V1 (vector PDF, separate attachment)
Print size28 cm wide x 20 cm high on size M; same size on all sizes
PlacementCentred, top of artwork 8 cm below front neck seam
ColoursOne colour, off-white on black body (give a Pantone reference or send a swatch)

Show the artwork on the flat sketch with the 8 cm measurement drawn in. If you want the print to scale by size, say so and give the size for each one.

Why it matters: "print on chest" means something different to every printer. Measured placement makes it repeatable across sizes and reorders. If you are still choosing a method, compare screen printing, DTF and DTG.

Page 7: Labels and packing

  • Neck label: printed inside back neck, brand logo and size, 6 cm wide, centred 2 cm below neck seam.
  • Care label: fibre content, wash care symbols, country of origin, inside left side seam 8 cm above hem.
  • Hang tag: attached at left sleeve hem with a tag pin or string, if you use one.
  • Fold: folded with print facing out, size label visible.
  • Polybag: one piece per polybag with size sticker; state your polybag type if you have a preference.
  • Cartons: packed by size, with carton marking showing style number, colour, size and quantity.

Why it matters: labels and packing are often the last thing a new brand thinks about and the first thing that delays dispatch. Custom labels can have their own minimums and lead times, so order them early.

What to change for your own style

  • Regular fit tee: reduce half chest and shoulder, raise the shoulder seam to the natural shoulder, shorten body length slightly.
  • Hoodie or sweatshirt: add hood height and width, cuff and waistband rib measurements, pocket position, drawcord and eyelet details. See our hoodie guide.
  • Embroidery instead of print: give stitch count or ask the embroiderer for it, thread colours, and backing type.
  • All-over or sleeve prints: add a placement drawing for each panel.

Before you send it

  1. Check every page has the same style number and version.
  2. Check every measuring point has a value for every size and a tolerance.
  3. Attach artwork as separate files, not only as pictures inside the PDF.
  4. Send it with your quantity and target date so the factory can quote properly.

Then follow the sampling process: review the first sample against this tech pack point by point and issue V2 with changes. Share your tech pack with Voz Cruda and we will match it with a suitable manufacturer.

Frequently asked questions

Can I copy this tech pack example for my own t-shirt?

Yes, use it as a starting structure. Replace the measurements with ones taken from a reference garment you like, and change the fabric, artwork and labels to match your product. The example numbers are illustrative, not a standard size chart.

What measurements are needed for a t-shirt tech pack?

At minimum: half chest, body length from high point of shoulder, shoulder width, sleeve length, sleeve opening, armhole, neck width, front neck drop, rib width and hem width, each with a tolerance.

How do I specify print placement in a tech pack?

Give the artwork size in centimetres and measure its position from a fixed point on the garment, such as 8 cm below the front neck seam and centred. Add a drawing showing the placement on the flat sketch.

Should the size chart be in centimetres or inches?

Either works if you are consistent, but most Indian garment factories work in centimetres or accept both. State the unit at the top of the size chart.

Does the factory need my tech pack in a specific format?

No. A clear PDF is the safest choice because it keeps the layout fixed. Send the artwork as separate high-resolution or vector files alongside it.
